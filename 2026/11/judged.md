By {{page.author}} on {{page.pubDate}}.

This week feels, personally, very much like an ‘in between’ week.

The Liturgical year is coming towards its concluding note of Triumph with our celebrations of Christ the King! And then we shall begin watching and waiting, as Advent heralds the coming of this same King - first as a baby boy born in Bethlehem but also as the wonderous risen and ascended saviour who sits at the right hand of God the Father Almighty, whence ‘he shall come to judge the quick and the dead.’ Sometimes in the midst of the Christmas Trees and Lights and Tinsel we can lose sight of this second coming.

I’ve been reflecting on this as my sight has required various bits of paperwork and so on to be completed and reading the reports about what I can and can’t see or do reliably has been quite a stark experience. Yet the clarity has not been unkind, simply realistic and, in so far as it is possible, objective. Although I am very good at coping with and managing my circumstances they remain challenging and that is what is acknowledged.

This has prompted me to reflect on what it may be like ‘to be judged’.

I don’t mean by friends and family, or people in our communities. I don’t mean in the way that people act judgementally on social media or after a couple of drinks. Rather, I wonder what it will be like to be in the presence of God on the Lord’s Day when he is holding up a mirror to our lives. A mirror, not a highlights reel or a summary or a well-written reference as if we were applying for a job. Rather a simple and objective reflection of the reality of our lives. Every rude thought, every act of kindness, every moment where we lost our temper or gave into temptation, and each time we sacrificed something for someone else, or performed an unseen act of service.

A ledger of virtues, if you will. Unflinching, uncompromising; an honest assessment.

I suspect that I will find that an uncomfortable experience; likely shying away from the reality of my sins and wanting to make excuses for them, even as I dismiss the value of the good things I have done. Yet in the midst of the discomfort my hope is that I shall understand myself as God understands me. That I shall discover through a profound understanding of my sinfulness the immense riches of God’s love and forgiveness at work in my life. For as the Prayerbook says, “…although we be unworthy, through our manifold sins, to offer unto thee any sacrifice, yet we beseech thee to accept this our bounden duty and service; not weighing our merits, but pardoning our offences, through Jesus Christ our Lord…”

For it is in Christ Jesus that we have been known and loved by God our Father, and by the presence of the Holy Spirit as a seal in our hearts that we are known as the Children of God - not because of the ‘good things’ that we try and do (in the hopes that they will outweigh the bad), but because in Christ we are forgiven and the stain of our offences are removed from the ledger.

Our sins are understood. They have consequences. And yet God in his infinite mercy has chosen to nail them to the cross that in the death of Christ they themselves might die with him. In the resurrection, Christ rises and the power of sin is revealed to have been undone and unmade. So we are not loved ‘in spite of’ our sins, we are loved such that our sins are removed as far from us as the east is from the west, and we discover that there is nowhere in all God’s creation where we can escape from his loving call upon our lives.

To be judged will be a strange experience and the day of judgement shall come upon us. So as we dwell in this ‘in between’ time, let us ensure that we bring our hearts before Christ the King and know him as our Lord and Saviour even as we turn towards Advent, watching and waiting for the return of the King.

> Every blessing,
> **Samuel S. Thorp**

p.p.s. The full quote from the Prayerbook reads: 

> O Lord and heavenly Father, we thy humble servants entirely desire thy fatherly goodness mercifully to accept this our sacrifice of praise and thanksgiving; most humbly beseeching thee to grant, that by the merits and death of thy Son Jesus Christ, and through faith in his blood, we and all thy whole Church may obtain remission of our sins, and all other benefits of his passion. And here we offer and present unto thee, O Lord, ourselves, our souls and bodies, to be a reasonable, holy, and lively sacrifice unto thee; humbly beseeching thee, that all we, who are partakers of this holy Communion, may be fulfilled with thy grace and heavenly benediction. And although we be unworthy, through our manifold sins, to offer unto thee any sacrifice, yet we beseech thee to accept this our bounden duty and service; not weighing our merits, but pardoning our offences, through Jesus Christ our Lord; by whom, and with whom, in the unity of the Holy Ghost, all honour and glory be unto thee, O Father Almighty, world without end. Amen.
